# Image-first article

Image-first opens one image-led article in the shared image modal. The full-height image stage is followed by centered article content; the ordinary article underneath retains its own state.

The image keeps its maximum available width and the article panel stays at up to
800 px. The panel has 40 px of space above and below; at viewport widths up to
600 px, both margins use the existing narrow image-first control spacing of
20 px. Its corners use `--border_radius`, the project's large radius (10 px by
default, matching the ordinary big-card panel). The same token rounds the image
host and its foreground image when the existing `image-modal-content-scrolled`
state is active; at the start position both retain zero radius. The scrolled
foreground's box follows its intrinsic photo ratio within the same
viewport-height host: square, portrait and landscape photos keep the centered
contain size, including enlargement of small sources. The radius therefore
clips the photo's painted corners rather than unused letterbox space. The stage
builder supplies the direct foreground image's natural ratio after decoding;
the scrolled width is `min(host width, 100dvh × ratio)` and its height is automatic.
This caps both axes while enlarging tiny sources, including a 1×1 image. Grid
centering preserves the previous photo position. Initial modal preparation waits
for decoding; a newly selected scrolled photo has zero width until its ratio is
ready. Replacement, record handoff and modal teardown remove the ratio and
prevent late decoding from restoring it. Nested SVG/logo presentations retain
their own sizes. Backgrounds of
the first and last panel children, including disclosure headers at those edges,
inherit the appropriate corners. The panel and its disclosure sections allow
overflow, preserving focus rings and any floating controls. The media host keeps
its existing image clipping, and disclosure-height animation keeps its temporary
clipping; the modal viewport and entrance/exit clipping are unchanged.

A click on empty background beside either the image or the article closes the entire image-first view through the existing modal close lifecycle. Article text, original-photo links, disclosure controls and editors remain interactive. Dragging a text selection from the article into a gutter does not count as a background click. Narrow screens may have no gutter beside the article; the close control and Escape remain available. Changing records keeps the same modal and installs the new record's background behavior without document-level listeners.

Stage clicks, including the click generated by a tap, compare viewport coordinates
with the current photo's centered contain rectangle inside its image box. Empty
space closes the view whether the event reaches the stage, media host or reveal
container, before or after scrolling. Photo pixels and image controls keep the
view open; captions, credits and framed SVG/CSS-logo presentations retain their
own interactions. The stage click listener is removed on record or modal disposal.

The details section uses the existing `row_article_section_details` translation key. Reviewed runtime translations remain authoritative. When a site's response lacks that key, the frontend supplies the existing bootstrap wording in Finnish, English, Chinese and Cantonese instead of displaying the technical key. Changing the UI language updates an already-open section. This fallback does not create or rewrite translation rows.

At viewport widths of 700 px and below, record-switch buttons use 44 × 44 px
icon-only targets; their preview thumbnails are hidden. Revealed disabled image
arrows use the shared 0.28 alpha at every width; enabled arrows and hidden
states keep their existing visibility rules. Details place each wrapping label above its value in
IFAV, Page and ordinary articles. IFAV and Page retain their panel spacing and
corners. The shared [phone article layout](Frontend_Results_Cards_And_Articles.md#phone-article-layout)
owns this viewport boundary and the ordinary article's compact frame.

Classic inline media, IFAV and the Page modal use `installImageControlsIdle(root,
activeClass)` in `card_view/image_controls_idle.js`: activity reveals controls,
1200 ms of pointer inactivity hides them, hovering a control holds them, focus
reveals them, pointerleave schedules hiding, and teardown clears listeners and
timers. Classic's image arrows, position and record bar share its active class;
IFAV's arrows and top record buttons share the modal active class. Page currently
has a plain contained image and Close, with no image arrows or record bar; Close
inherits the same modal idle lifecycle. Keyboard focus still reveals controls.

On `(hover: none), (pointer: coarse)` inputs, classic controls remain visible
(including position and record bar); IFAV arrows, Close, record buttons and image
position remain visible at idle, with the scroll hint still suppressed after
70 px of scrolling. Page's Close stays visible on touch; it has no arrows or
record bar. The shared timer still expires its active class on touch; CSS owns
these visibility exceptions. Existing fades and reduced-motion rules are unchanged.

Disabled `.row_article_image_first_arrow` and `.big_card_thumbnail_carousel_button`
buttons share exactly one revealed-alpha rule in `row_article_image_controls.css`:
`--row-article-image-arrow-visible-alpha: 0.28`. Hidden arrows still resolve to
opacity 0. Disabled arrows retain the normal enabled resting colours, borders and
blur, with no shadow or hover change and a not-allowed cursor; their revealed hit
area remains intact. There is no disabled-opacity token in `variables.css`.
Keep this rule canonical; do not add another disabled opacity rule; the guard test enforces it.
`row_article_disabled_arrow_guard.test.js` parses every frontend stylesheet except
`dist` and checks disabled arrow compounds, including `:is()`/`:where()` branches.

The modal uses the application's explicit theme and existing reduced-motion behavior. Image captions and their original-photo attribution are described in [Article image captions](Article_Image_Captions.md).

Focused regression tests live beside `image_first_view_opener.js` and `translation_handler.js`; the image-stage and modal tests cover the existing close animation, image/letterbox boundary and ratio disposal, including a close during record handoff. Explicit stage/photo rectangles check clicks and simulated taps beside, above/below and on photos at desktop/phone sizes, both scroll states, image replacement and click-listener disposal. The CSS contract test checks both panel gaps (desktop and narrow screens), background corners, visible overflow and the scrolled-state image sizing/corners, with width- and height-limited square, portrait, landscape and 1×1 sources. The C10 browser flow measures, in its desktop project, the gap above and below the panel, the panel corners and the image corners before and after a real scroll. It checks the foreground box against the natural photo ratio and centered contain rectangle, briefly constraining/restoring only the media host to cover both size limits with its existing square fixture. Trailing space is measurable offscreen without an extra end/back scroll. Its foreground click keeps the view open, then the adjacent backdrop click proves the existing closing animation. Before cleanup, including after an assertion failure, C10 restores the source list address, verifies the image-first view is gone and waits for the card list. It does not wait for network idle because the live-update stream stays open. Browser verification should also include square, portrait and landscape photos at phone/desktop sizes, real article gutters, text selection, interactive content, language switches and both explicit themes.

## Page-sized image-first article (cifav)

The Page presentation keeps the stored key `image_first_page_view`. Its image
stays inside the text column, with its whole photo contained at full column
width and a maximum height of 80dvh above 700 px. At 700 px and below, it keeps
the original `min(40dvh, 360px)` cap; the existing phone layout rules still apply.

Only cifav adds 24 px below the image box; the title retains its 19 px top
padding (43 px from the image box to the title content). The title has no bottom
margin, leaving the description's existing 20 px top padding as the single gap
before text. The first rich-text child's top margin is zero, whether it is a
heading or paragraph. Later headings keep their normal section spacing. These
overrides do not change ordinary Article or IFAV presentation.

While cifav is open, a shared background-media hold pauses Home's background
video, including both copies during a cross-fade. Close, navigation (including
Back/Forward) and replacement release its hold once. Other hold reasons keep
playback paused until the last release. Playback resumes only if it was playing
when held (or Home mounted during the hold), remains mounted and reduced motion is
off; an already paused video stays paused. IFAV and ordinary Article take no hold. The player unsubscribes
when Home is cleaned up. Focused media/history tests prove these exit paths;
C26 uses the width-dependent contained-image limit for browser verification.

## Choosing a presentation in the view picker

The dataset view picker ("Show content as…", between Cards and Table) shows
Article as one joined split button (`table_views/row_presentation/`). The main
button's caption is the presentation chosen for this dataset in this browser
tab, in the UI language: Article (`view_article`, default), Image-first article
(`view_image_first`, served from the view registry's local fallbacks) or
Page-sized image-first article (`article_presentations_page`). The chevron is
named by `article_presentations_title` and opens the shared anchored action
menu (`reusable_components/action_menu/`), which marks the current choice with
a check mark. Choosing an item stores it (`<dataset>_row_presentation` in tab
session storage; no server default changes), updates the caption and opens it;
a later primary click opens the same choice. Only this picker gets the split:
the chart fallback notice keeps the plain Article button, and the More menu,
default-view controls, Home buttons and photo openers are unchanged.

- **Article** uses the picker's existing classic path unchanged.
- **IFAV and Page** open over the current listing, which stays the history
  origin: Close, Escape and Back return to it; Forward and reload reopen.
  The row is the open classic article's row; otherwise the first row of a fresh
  request limited to one row (`filters.limit: 1`), with the listing's own
  filters, committed search and sort. Those listing conditions are checked
  through record loading, rendering and the final history commit.
  Every primary click or menu choice, Article included, is recorded at once,
  before any lazy loading; a newer choice in any dataset, a listing whose
  conditions changed meanwhile, any application address write (push or replace,
  including the same address), view deactivation or any history move cancels
  an opening that has not committed. A document-wide write revision makes
  cancellation independent of when the pending request samples the address;
  returning to its original address and history entry cannot revive it.
  The opener checks once before its own synchronous history commit, which
  finishes that opening without rechecking its revision afterward.
  An empty listing opens nothing and shows the existing
  `no_results` notice; an opening that fails or whose record is no longer
  readable shows the generic `load_failed` notice, naming no row.
- **Rights.** Article and IFAV need the Article grant (IFAV keeps the Article
  gate) and Page its own route. A dataset whose default view is Article grants
  nothing here, because the classic row opener refuses Article without the
  grant. The menu lists only permitted items and hides the chevron when one
  remains, so a Page-only reader sees Page as the main caption and never passes
  the classic gate. No grant is widened; the server still decides the row.
- **Fresh record.** Only the row id travels: the opener fetches the permitted
  record and its current fields (`presentationChoice`), as history restore does.
- **Text-only IFAV.** A chosen IFAV whose readable row has no permitted photo
  opens without a stage (`image_first_view--text-only`). The history entry keeps
  `presentationChoice: true`, so reload and Forward reopen it text-only; an entry
  opened from a photo whose photo is gone still returns to its origin, and a
  denied or failed record opens nothing. A copied address in a new tab has no
  history state and follows the photo-click rule. Page never needed a photo.

The menu is portalled to `<body>` with fixed positioning and the shared
`measureFloatingPanelPlacement` rule, so neither the viewport nor a transformed
sidebar clips it. It supports Enter, Space, the arrow keys, Home, End, Escape
(focus returns to the chevron) and Tab (closes without consuming the key, so
native focus traversal continues from the chevron); a pointer outside, leaving the view or
removing the toolbar closes it, and its document listeners exist only while it
is open. Choosing an item does not count as a click outside the phone filter
panel, so the panel stays open and focus can return to the split button when
the presentation closes. Dialog ownership is on the disposable image-first
content, never the shared overlay; close and reuse remove it. The outside-click
listener captures picker ownership before a close handler removes the content,
while ordinary and photo dialogs retain their existing dismissal behaviour.
Rows are 44 px high and wrap long translated labels. Unit tests live
beside these modules and in `image_first_view_presentation_choice.test.js`; the
browser proof is `C32_row_presentation_choice.spec.ts`.

## Ordinary article result lists

Opening an ordinary article from a paginated result view transfers its currently
loaded row prefix to the compact article list. The list continues at the next
unread offset using its source view's field projection; it does not replay pages.
The expanded article independently fetches its current permitted fields and types.
The compact list has its own scroll observer. Repeated boundary rows are deduplicated
without changing the server offset. Back restores the retained source list and its
offset; Forward reuses that list before continuing article-side pagination.
Changing filters, language or access invalidates incompatible transfers. Intelligent
search retains its separate streaming lifecycle and does not request ordinary pages.
The result count remains before the result list, including after article return and
further page appends.
